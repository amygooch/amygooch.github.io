Computer graphics and scientific visualization

Amy Ashurst Gooch

Chief Operations Officer, ViSUS and ViSOAR
Salt Lake City, Utah

I work on ways to keep the important part of a picture, a model, or a scientific dataset visible when the data is simplified, recolored, or too large to open on an ordinary computer.

I earned a B.S. in computer engineering and an M.S. in computer science at the University of Utah, and a Ph.D. in computer science at Northwestern University. The cool-to-warm lighting model from my master’s thesis, developed with Bruce Gooch, Peter Shirley, and Elaine Cohen, is the shading method often called Gooch shading. I later co-authored Non-Photorealistic Rendering with Bruce Gooch. I spent seven years on the faculty at the University of Victoria. Since 2013 I have led product and operations at ViSUS LLC, now renamed ViSOAR LLC, whose software is built on top of OpenVisus, so that visualization of massive data stays lightweight enough to run without specialized hardware.

Two lines of work

Massive scientific data

My work at ViSOAR LLC with OpenVisus from CEDMAV and the National Science Data Fabric democratizes access to gigbyte to petascale datasets: climate records, dark-matter calibration data, and other scientific archives that are awkward to move and awkward to view. Our focus is on multiresolution storage, web visualization, and FAIR access.

Pictures and perception

The earlier work asks which differences in a scene should survive when an image is drawn, converted to gray, or shown in a display that cannot carry every cue. Technical illustration, Color2Gray, facial illustration, and studies of distance in virtual environments all sit on that question.

Cool-to-warm shading

Diffuse shading loses the small claws in the black and the highlights in the white. A hue shift at nearly constant brightness keeps the edge lines visible, but the curved surfaces stay subtle. Cool-to-warm shading uses both a change in brightness and a blue-to-yellow shift, so the lines, the highlights, and the claws remain visible. Figures 4.13, 4.16, and 4.18 from the 1998 master’s thesis. Amy Gooch, Bruce Gooch, Peter Shirley, and Elaine Cohen published the model at ACM SIGGRAPH 1998.

Color2Gray

Claude Monet, Impression, Sunrise. A luminance-only grayscale loses the sun and its reflection. Color2Gray folds visible color changes back into the gray image. From Gooch, Olsen, Tumblin, and Gooch, ACM SIGGRAPH 2005.
